Virgin Voyages vs. Royal Caribbean for a first cruise

Two very different answers to the same question. Which one you'll prefer comes down almost entirely to whether you want children on the ship.

This is the most common question I get from people who've never cruised, and it's usually asked the wrong way round. People ask which line is better. Neither is better. They're built for different weeks.

So here's the version that actually helps.

The one question that decides it

Do you want children on the ship?

Virgin Voyages is adults-only. Eighteen and over, no exceptions, on every sailing. Royal Caribbean is one of the most family-oriented lines afloat, with kids' programming that starts at six months on some ships.

That single fact drives almost everything else — the noise level, the pool deck, the entertainment, the dining rooms, the time the ship gets quiet at night. If you have a strong feeling either way, you've already chosen, and the rest of this is detail.

What Virgin does differently

The thing people notice first is what's already in the fare. Gratuities are included. Basic wifi is included. So is a long list of restaurants that would carry a surcharge elsewhere, plus group fitness classes and the still and sparkling water in your cabin. You'll still spend money on board — bar tabs and shore excursions are yours — but the running tally is shorter than you expect.

The second thing is the absence of things. No formal night. No assigned dining time or fixed table. No buffet in the traditional sense. No photographers positioned at the gangway. If cruising has always sounded slightly regimented to you, this is the version that removes most of what you were reacting to.

The ships are also mid-sized rather than enormous, which changes the feel more than the deck plan suggests. You learn the layout on day one instead of day three.

What Royal Caribbean does differently

Scale, and what scale buys. The largest ships in the fleet carry features that simply cannot exist on a smaller vessel — full waterparks, ice rinks, skydiving simulators, multi-story slides, whole outdoor neighborhoods with trees in them. If you're traveling with anyone under sixteen, this is a very hard proposition to beat.

The trade is that the fleet spans an enormous range. A brand-new flagship and a smaller, older ship in the same fleet are close to different vacations at different prices. This is exactly where people get it wrong booking alone: they pick the line, then the itinerary, then take whatever ship is on that date.

Royal Caribbean also does the traditional cruise things Virgin has deliberately dropped. There's a main dining room with an assigned option, there are dress-up nights if you want them, there's a proper buffet. For a lot of first-timers that familiarity is a feature, not a compromise.

The short version

  • Choose Virgin if you want adults-only, a shorter final bill, and none of the formality.
  • Choose Royal Caribbean if you're bringing kids, want the biggest ships afloat, or like the classic cruise structure.
  • Either works for a first cruise. Neither is a mistake. They're just aimed at different people.

Where the price comparison misleads you

Comparing headline fares between these two lines is close to meaningless, because they include different things. A Virgin fare with gratuities, wifi and most dining baked in is not comparable to a Royal Caribbean fare where those are separate lines.

The number to compare is what you'll actually have spent by the time you get off. For a couple, that usually means adding gratuities, a drinks package if you want one, specialty dining, and wifi to the Royal Caribbean fare before you put the two side by side. Sometimes Virgin wins that arithmetic and sometimes it doesn't — but the raw fares will tell you the wrong thing either way.

The thing neither website will tell you

Your cabin matters more than your line. A well-placed cabin on the "wrong" ship beats a badly placed one on the "right" ship, every time. Deck position, what's directly above and below you, how far you are from the elevators, whether an "obstructed view" means a rope or an entire lifeboat — none of that is visible when you're booking, and all of it is knowable.

That's the part I'd rather spend your first conversation on. More on cabin categories here.

So which would I put you on?

If you're a couple with no children in the party, no strong attachment to cruise tradition, and a slight suspicion that you might not like cruising — Virgin, most of the time. It's the version that converts skeptics, and the all-in fare removes the nickel-and-diming that puts people off.

If you're traveling as a family, or with a mixed-age group, or you actively want the big-ship spectacle — Royal Caribbean, and then a real conversation about which ship, because that's where the decision actually lives.
