Ways to travel

Three things, done properly

I've narrowed what I plan to cruises, all-inclusive resorts, and small group tours — plus the honeymoons and LGBTQ+ travel that sit on top of them.

Plenty of advisors will book you anything. In practice that means broad familiarity and deep knowledge of almost nothing. Which is fine right up until the moment it matters — usually the moment you're choosing between two cabins or two room categories, and nobody can tell you the difference.

So I stopped. These three are what I know properly: I've sailed the ships, walked the resorts, and know which tour operators enforce their group caps. Everything below is organized by how you travel rather than where, because in my experience that's the decision that actually determines whether you enjoy the trip.

What if I want something else?

Ask. Plenty of trips are adjacent — a land package either side of a sailing, a family celebration that needs eight rooms, a rail journey. If it's close to what I do well, I'll take it on. If it isn't, I'll point you to someone who does it properly — because you deserve the specialist, not the generalist.
