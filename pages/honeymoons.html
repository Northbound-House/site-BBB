What shape should your honeymoon take?

The resort honeymoon

One place, one unpacking, and a room category chosen properly. This is the right answer for most couples, and the whole thing turns on the room. Swim-up access, a butler tier, a quieter section of beach, a club lounge that's actually worth using. At the Sandals and Hyatt Inclusive properties I'm certified with, moving up a tier changes the trip more than changing resort would.

The cruise honeymoon

Several places, one unpacking. Underrated for honeymoons, and particularly good if you're the kind of couple who'd get restless after four days on one beach. The cabin decision matters more than usual here — a balcony earns its money on a honeymoon in a way it doesn't on a family sailing.

The combination

A week's sailing followed by four or five nights at a resort to decompress before flying home. It's the most-requested thing I book in this category, and it's genuinely the best of both if you have the days.

The perks — and why timing decides whether you get them

Nearly every cruise line and resort group runs a honeymoon or celebration program. Almost none of it happens automatically. The room upgrade. The bottle on arrival. The private dinner on the beach, the spa credit, the late checkout on your last day. Somebody has to request every one of those against your booking — often weeks out, sometimes with documentation.

Mentioning it at check-in gets you a card on the pillow. Having it flagged at booking, chased at thirty days, and reconfirmed the week before is how you actually get the upgrade. That's the part I do.

On top of that, Signature Travel Network benefits stack on many properties and sailings — resort credit, onboard credit, breakfast, early check-in. I'll tell you exactly what's available on your booking rather than what's theoretically possible.

Timing, which nobody plans for

Two things people consistently get wrong.

The first is leaving the day after the wedding. It sounds romantic and it is genuinely brutal — you'll spend the first two days of an expensive trip asleep. A buffer day, or a "minimoon" now and the real trip three months later, is almost always the better call.

The second is names. Your flights and your cruise booking must match your passport exactly. If you're changing your name, that process takes longer than you think. A booking in a name your passport doesn't carry is a problem at a check-in desk, not a paperwork detail. Book in your current legal name and we'll deal with it properly.
